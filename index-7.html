Where the money leaks / retrofit-specific

A retrofit business loses margin in four places. Three of them are logistics.

High-value electronics, model-and-VIN specific fitment, two shipping origins and a technical support team that also fields "it doesn't work" calls. These are my working assumptions — I would confirm each one against your data in week one, then fix in this order.

Leak 01 · fitment

Wrong-part returns

On a fit-dependent catalogue, most returns are not faults — they are a compatibility promise the product page did not keep. Every one costs you outbound freight, inbound freight, a restock check and a support ticket.

I have done this fix. Returns at 35% were mostly sizing. Rewriting the sizing content and the policy behind it — not the courier contract — took returns to 5%.

Leak 02 · routing

Shipping from the wrong hub

With Warsaw and Orlando both holding stock, every order has a right and a wrong origin. Get the rule wrong and you pay duty, long-haul freight and a five-day lead time on a part sitting 200 km from the customer.

What I would build. A route-to-hub rule by destination, value and stock position, with cost per order and landed cost measured per lane — not per courier invoice.

Leak 03 · RMA

Returns that were never faulty

Coding, firmware and installer error look identical to a defect on a returns form. If the RMA opens before technical triage, you pay freight twice to receive a working module.

The mechanic. A self-serve returns portal with structured reason codes and a technical-triage gate. Structured reasons are what let me see the 35% was sizing in the first place.

Leak 04 · cover

Long-lead modules out of stock

Hardware with a long supplier lead time and demand that spikes on a model-year release will go out of stock at exactly the wrong moment, in exactly one of the two hubs.

Where I have run it. Buy depth and stock cover across five condition grades at Noon Renewed, priced by grade to clear at target margin and hold availability.

Case study / Genes Lecoanet Hemant · UAE launch

How I took a brand from no UAE presence to shipping orders, in under four months.

A premium fashion house running out of India wanted a UAE business. There was no warehouse, no 3PL, no local storefront, no team. The order below is the whole point — logistics first, because everything after it depends on stock actually being in market.

Step 01 · supply side first

Contracted the 3PL before anything else

Ran supplier selection and signed Aramex to hold stock in the UAE — pricing, terms, delivery schedules and returns handling agreed up front. You cannot promise a delivery date you have not bought.

Step 02 · cross-border

Moved stock into market

Coordinated the international shipment of inventory into the UAE and positioned it at the 3PL, so the first order shipped domestically rather than from another country.

Step 03 · in parallel

Migrated the storefront while goods were in transit

Full domain migration to a separate UAE storefront with Stripe and Telr as local payment gateways, done during transit time instead of after it. No dead weeks.

Step 04 · demand side

Onboarded Namshi as a trusted channel

A local marketplace with existing trust does the customer-acquisition work a new domain cannot. First orders in launch week came through it.

Step 05 · October 2025

Live — first orders shipped in launch week

From standing start to fulfilled orders in under four months, including hiring the first UAE roles and briefing developers on systems and roadmap.

Step 06 · weeks after

Then iterated, one bottleneck at a time

Return Prime self-serve returns portal replacing inbound service calls, a rewards programme, same-day delivery, and try-on-at-the-door with alternate sizes. Each one shipped because a number said it was the next constraint.

Running now

150–200 UAE orders a month, alongside 2,000–2,500 in India

Two markets, one weekly operating rhythm, one person accountable for both. Over the same period the India business grew 5x from INR 20 lakh to INR 1 crore a month while the offline estate shrank from six stores to three.

The transferable one / 35% → 5%

The returns fix was never a logistics fix. That is why it works on retrofits too.

Fashion returns on sizing. Retrofits return on fitment. Same failure, same repair sequence — the promise on the product page is wrong, and the warehouse pays for it twice.

What I did at Genes Lecoanet Hemant
Symptom

35% of orders coming back. Reverse logistics eating margin and clogging customer service with inbound calls.

Repair

Named reverse logistics as the largest single cost bottleneck. Rewrote the returns policy and the sizing content behind it. Replaced calls with a Return Prime self-serve portal that captures a structured reason on every return. Returns fell to 5% with gross margin protected — and every return now feeds the next fix.

The same loop at BimmerTech
Symptom to test in week one

Split the returns file by reason. My expectation: a large share is fitment or configuration, not hardware failure — wrong variant for the model, year, trim or existing option code.

Repair

Tighten the compatibility promise where it is made, gate the RMA behind a technical triage step so working modules stop travelling, and put structured reason codes on every return so Sales, Technical Support and logistics argue from the same file. Then measure returns rate and cost per order weekly, per hub.
